    The concept

    A group trip planner built around two ideas: the itinerary should reflect everyone’s preferences, not just the loudest person’s, and splitting costs afterward shouldn’t be a math problem. Each member sets an energy level and interests, and Settled generates an itinerary from the group’s combined preferences and budget. During the trip, members log expenses, either shared with chosen people, which feeds the settle-up, or kept personal just for tracking.

    How it works

    • Live voting on every slot: a majority downvote swaps it for the next-best activity, a majority upvote locks it in, with no admin step
    • “Why this pick”: a Gemini-generated note per slot explaining who an activity suits, and honestly who it suits less, with a graceful fallback if the API fails
    • A greedy debt-simplification algorithm settles up with the minimum number of payments

    The concept

    A restaurant reservation platform with a front-end-only architecture: everything persists in the browser’s localStorage. Guests create a profile, get recommendations, pick a table from a photo of the dining room, pre-order, invite friends to split the bill, and manage bookings from their own dashboard.

    How it works

    • A rule-based pricing engine: peak and off-peak rates from each restaurant’s own hours, coupon codes and a loyalty discount
    • A recommendation engine scores restaurants against cuisine, dietary and allergy preferences, and always ranks allergy conflicts last
    • Tables are picked on real floor photos, with clickable hotspots colored by live availability
    • Group bookings: invite friends, track RSVPs, and the bill splits automatically among the guests who accept
    • A waitlist for fully booked slots, and a booking cap to prevent runaway reservations
